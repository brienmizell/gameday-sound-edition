2018 → 2026

The first app, and the same app eight years later.

← Back to GameDay

gameDay was built in October 2018 over eight days at DigitalCrafts by Brien Mizell and Evan Procter — 119 commits, four files, the first thing either of them shipped. It fetched a week of college football and chased the weather at every stadium: stadium name → Google Geocode → latitude and longitude → Dark Sky. That three-hop chain was the idea, and it was a good one.

Every service it depended on is now gone. The panel on the left is the original code, unmodified, running against real 2018 Week 10 results — the exact week it was hardcoded to.

What actually changed
2018 2026
Schedule FantasyData v3, via a Heroku CORS proxy Public ESPN scoreboard — keyless, CORS-open
Geocoding Google Geocode, key in source Open-Meteo geocoder, and it disambiguates by state
Weather Dark Sky — Apple shut it down in March 2023 Open-Meteo, keyless
CORS my-little-cors-proxy.herokuapp.com — died with Heroku's free tier None needed; both APIs send access-control-allow-origin: *
API keys in source 3, committed to a public repo 0
Week var page = 10. Prev/Next buttons commented out, never wired Full season calendar, arrow keys, linkable in the URL
Which day's weather daily.data[4] — the 5th day of a rolling forecast, whatever day the game was Matched to the game's own date; says so when the game is past the 16-day horizon
Conference filter Built, then deleted before submission Power 4 by default, all eleven FBS conferences available
Team search Threw on every keystroke — its list was commented out Filters teams, stadiums, and cities
Per game 8 fields: two names, two scores, stadium, city, state, date Adds rank, record, logo, team colors, TV, betting line, dome and neutral-site flags, kickoff in your timezone, countdown
Cupcakes — FCS opponents and 21+ spreads marked, and hideable
Failure A slow geocode took the whole card with it Weather fills in after the slate renders; a card never waits on it
Music — A Sound slot on every card, waiting on the music system
Code 608 lines, 133 of them commented-out ~2,500 lines across 8 modules, no build step, no dependencies
